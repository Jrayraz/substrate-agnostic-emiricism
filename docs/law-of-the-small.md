# The Law of the Small

The Law of the Small is the foundational principle of Substrate-Agnostic Empiricism.  
It states that **microsignals**—the smallest observable units of change—contain the earliest detectable evidence of larger patterns.

## Definition

A **microsignal** is any minimal, observable fluctuation in a system.  
Examples include:
- a neuron firing  
- a micro‑movement in muscle tissue  
- a sensor reading shift  
- a tiny geometric deformation  
- a market tick  
- a micro‑event in spacetime curvature  

Microsignals are not meaningful individually.  
They become meaningful when they **repeat**.

## Core Claim

> Repeating microsignals form patterns.  
> Patterns scale upward into the structures we call “reality.”

This means:
- physics emerges from repeating physical microsignals  
- biology emerges from repeating biochemical microsignals  
- cognition emerges from repeating neural microsignals  
- social systems emerge from repeating behavioral microsignals  

## Why “Small” Matters

Large-scale events are slow, noisy, and often misleading.  
Microsignals:
- appear first  
- repeat faster  
- reveal structure earlier  
- show direction before macro‑events form  

Thus, the Law of the Small provides a **predictive advantage**:  
patterns can be detected before they fully emerge.

## Implications

- The smallest observable unit is the most informative.  
- Pattern detection begins at the micro-level.  
- Substrate does not matter; repetition does.  
- Microsignals unify domains that appear unrelated.
