# Core Hypotheses

Substrate-Agnostic Empiricism proposes that reality is composed of repeating patterns detectable through microsignals.  
These hypotheses form the foundation of the field.

---

## Hypothesis 1: Existence Is Pattern Repetition
All observable phenomena—physical, biological, cognitive, social—are expressions of repeating patterns found across time and space.

Patterns are not domain-specific; they are universal structures.

---

## Hypothesis 2: Microsignals Reveal Patterns First
The smallest observable fluctuations (microsignals) contain the earliest evidence of emerging patterns.

Microsignals repeat before macro-events form, making them the most predictive units of observation.

---

## Hypothesis 3: Substrate Is Secondary
The medium (neurons, molecules, spacetime curvature, markets, behaviors) does not determine the pattern.

Patterns remain consistent across substrates, suggesting that the substrate is a carrier, not a cause.

---

## Hypothesis 4: Cross-Domain Pattern Equivalence
Patterns in physics, biology, neurobiology, and social systems often share identical structures:
- oscillations  
- cascades  
- feedback loops  
- attractors  
- symmetry breaks  
- phase transitions  
- self-similarity  

This equivalence implies a unified underlying architecture.

---

## Hypothesis 5: Reality Can Be Modeled Through Pattern Taxonomy
If patterns are universal, then a cross-domain taxonomy can describe reality independent of substrate.

This taxonomy would allow:
- prediction  
- modeling  
- comparison  
- simulation  

…across any domain using the same pattern types.
